This thread explains how validator voting power works today and how it is expected to evolve.

───────────

**Current phase — Bootstrap**
- At launch, a large portion of the total stake is held by the founders.
- Validators own their keys and their stake.
- No validator stake is ever removed, modified, or confiscated.
- Validator voting power is mainly influenced by founders' delegation distribution.
- If relative voting power changes, it happens only because additional stake is delegated elsewhere, not because anything is taken away.

───────────

**Slashing & downtime (current parameters)**
- Downtime: jail 600 seconds (manual unjail)
- Slashing: 0%

Both parameters are governance-controlled and can evolve through DAO decisions.
There is no discretionary slashing and no validator-specific punishment.

───────────

**Decentralization roadmap**
The founders stake is not intended to remain dominant.

**Phase 1 — Bootstrap**
- Validator onboarding
- ~25% of founders stake delegated
- Focus: liveness and operational validation

**Phase 2 — Infrastructure & product delivery**
- IPFS, Gateways and browser shipped
- Network distribution layer goes live
- Founders retain operational flexibility

**Phase 3 — Network opening**
- Additional ~25% of founders stake delegated
- Validator set participation increases
- Reduced reliance on founders-controlled stake

**Phase 4 — Stabilization**
- Progressive reduction of founders-controlled stake
- Increasing community delegation
- Founders influence decreases over time

**Final goal**
- Founders stake fully withdrawn
- Validator voting power driven entirely by community stake
- Founders act only as protocol contributors

**Key takeaway**
This is a transitional bootstrap model, not a permanent control mechanism.
No validator is economically locked in, and all dynamics follow normal staking rules.
